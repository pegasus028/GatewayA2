## The pattern
**Look at the body, not the name**

Scientists group animals by their bodies, not by their names or homes. Feathers, wings and a beak = bird: eagle, owl, and penguin, which can't fly but swims with its wings. Six legs = insect: bee, butterfly. Eight legs = the spider group (arachnids), so a scorpion is not an insect. Fins, and it breathes in water = fish: shark. Breathes air and feeds its babies milk = mammal: whale, hippo, rhino, monkey, rat, tiger. Dry skin with scales = reptile: snake, lizard. No bones and no brain: jellyfish, so it is not a fish! Smaller clues help too: horns (rhino), a sting (bee, scorpion, jellyfish), stripes (tiger), spots (leopard), thick fur (bear, wolf, fox).

Examples:
- A **penguin** is a bird that can't fly.
- A **whale** is a mammal, but a **shark** is a fish.
- A **scorpion** has eight legs, so it isn't an insect.

Unit words that follow this pattern: eagle, owl, penguin, bee, butterfly, scorpion, shark, whale, hippo, rhino, monkey, rat, tiger, snake, lizard, jellyfish, horn, leopard, bear, wolf, fox.
