## The pattern
**Don't trust the spelling**

Some food words don't sound the way they look. Silent letters: yoghurt has no /h/ sound (YOG-ut) and biscuit has no /u/ sound (BIS-kit). Tricky endings: sausage and cabbage end in /ɪdʒ/ (SOS-ij, CAB-ij), lettuce ends in /ɪs/ (LET-is) and spinach in /ɪtʃ/. Onion and honey have the vowel of 'cup' in the first syllable (UN-yun, HUN-ee). In pizza, zz sounds like /ts/. Stress usually falls on the first syllable: APple, CARrot, ONion, SALad, SUgar, BROCcoli, CUcumber, MUSHroom. Watch out for toMAto (British to-MAH-to) and lemoNADE. Many learners stress the last syllable, so practise these aloud.

Examples:
- YOG-ut — the h is silent.
- SOS-ij and CAB-ij — -age says /ɪdʒ/.
- LET-is — not 'let-toos'.
- to-MAH-to, lem-o-NADE

Unit words that follow this pattern: yoghurt, biscuit, sausage, cabbage, lettuce, spinach, onion, honey, pizza, tomato, lemonade, broccoli, cucumber, mushroom, salad.
