## This stage in one sentence
Suggest, accept, say no politely and fix the day, time and place.

The stage has 2 modules, in this order: (1) Suggestions; (2) Accepting, rejecting and arranging.

## Module 1: Suggestions
Student's Book p.100 · level A2

**The key idea:** Let's go! / Shall we go? / Why don't we go? / We could go — all + base verb. But What about + -ing: What about going?

Five ways to suggest an idea (Speaking bank p.100). Four of them take the base verb: *Let's **go**, *Shall we **go**?*, *Why don't we **go**?*, *We could **go**.

*What about* is different: it takes a noun or a verb + *-ing*: *What about **the zoo**?* *What about **going** to the zoo?* (✗ *What about go…?*)

Watch the small words: no *to* after *Let's* (✗ *Let's to go*). *Shall we…?* and *Why don't we…?* are questions — keep the question order: ✗ *Why we don't go?*

Pattern table:
- Expression | + | Example
- Let's | base verb | Let's go swimming.
- Shall we | base verb + ? | Shall we meet at 2?
- Why don't we / you | base verb + ? | Why don't we take the BTS?
- We could | base verb | We could have a picnic.
- What about | noun or verb-ing + ? | What about playing badminton?

Good examples:
- **Why don't we go** to the night market on Friday?
- **We could watch** a film at my house.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "What about go to the beach?" → Right: "What about going to the beach?"
- Wrong: "Let's to meet at 5." → Right: "Let's meet at 5."
- Wrong: "Why don't we going bowling?" → Right: "Why don't we go bowling?"

Memory tip: Only What about wants -ing. The other four want the plain verb.

## Module 2: Accepting, rejecting and arranging
Student's Book pp.100–101 · level A2

**The key idea:** Say yes, or say no politely, then fix the day, time and place: Sorry, I'm busy on Saturday. Are you free on Sunday?

Accepting (Speaking bank p.100): *Yes, sure. / That's fine. / OK. / Great. / Good idea.* Rejecting politely: *Sorry, I can't. / Sorry, I'm busy. / Thanks, but…* — *Thanks, but…* sounds friendly, but it means no!

Give a reason with the present continuous: *Sorry, I can't. I'm visiting my grandma on Saturday.* Then suggest another time: *Are you free on Sunday?*

Fix the details: *Let's meet **at** 2 pm **at** the bowling alley.* Use *on* + day (*on Friday*) and *at* + time (*at 3*).

In a short message (p.101), answer every point in the task — where, when and how you'll travel. A missing point loses marks.

Pattern table:
- Job | Useful expressions
- Ask about plans | Are you free on…? · Are you doing anything at…? · Do you want to…? · How about you?
- Accept | Yes, sure. · That's fine. · OK. · Great. · Good idea.
- Reject | Sorry, I can't. · Sorry, I'm busy. · Thanks, but…
- Arrange | Let's meet at 4 at… · See you then!

Good examples:
- **Thanks, but** I don't really like horror films. What about a comedy?
- **Good idea!** Let's meet at 3 at the BTS station.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "Are you free in Sunday?" → Right: "Are you free on Sunday?"
- Wrong: "Sorry, I'm busy. I visit my aunt on Saturday." → Right: "Sorry, I'm busy. I'm visiting my aunt on Saturday."
- Wrong: "Do you want go to the cinema?" → Right: "Do you want to go to the cinema?"

Memory tip: Polite no = Sorry + reason + new idea: Sorry, I'm busy on Friday. Are you free on Saturday?

## Quick challenge for the end of the episode
Question: It's so hot today! Let's (blank) swimming at the pool.
Options: (A) go  (B) to go  (C) going  (D) goes
Answer: go — *Let's* + base verb: *Let's go swimming.* No *to* and no *-ing* after *Let's*.
