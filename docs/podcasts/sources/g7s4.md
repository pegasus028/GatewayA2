## This stage in one sentence
Find the clue, pick the form — and keep will out of when and if.

The stage has 2 modules, in this order: (1) Which future?; (2) when / if + present.

## Module 1: Which future?
Student's Book pp.94–102 · level B1

**The key idea:** Find the clue, then choose: decided before → going to; in the diary → present continuous; opinion → will.

English has no single “future tense”. You choose the form from the **clue** in the situation. Read everything first — the clue is often in another sentence: *I've saved my money…*, *We've got the tickets…*, *I think…*

Plan in your head (*I've decided, I've saved*) → *going to*. Fixed arrangement (time + place + other people, tickets) → present continuous. Your opinion (*I think, I'm sure, probably*) → *will*.

Sometimes two forms are both fine: *I'm meeting Ploy at 5* and *I'm going to meet Ploy at 5* are both correct. That's why every question here gives you a clear clue. The present simple (*I meet*) is usually wrong for personal plans.

Pattern table:
- Clue | Form | Example
- I've decided / I've saved / I want to… | going to | I've saved 500 baht. I'm going to buy a new game.
- time + place + person / tickets / diary | present continuous | I'm meeting Ken at MBK at 4 tomorrow.
- I think / I'm sure / probably / in 2050 | will | I think it'll be sunny tomorrow.
- Look! (evidence) — Extra | going to | Look at that cloud! It's going to rain.
- decision now / offer / promise — Extra | will | The phone's ringing. I'll answer it!

Good examples:
- We've got the tickets. We're **watching** the match at the stadium on Sunday.
- I'm sure you**'ll love** Chiang Mai.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "What do you do tomorrow evening?" → Right: "What are you doing tomorrow evening?"
- Wrong: "The phone's ringing. I answer it." → Right: "The phone's ringing. I'll answer it."
- Wrong: "I think it is raining tomorrow." → Right: "I think it'll rain tomorrow."

Extra: Extra clues from the Extra boxes: something you can see now (*Look at the sky!*) → *going to*; a decision, offer or promise made at the moment you speak → *will*.

Memory tip: Detective rule: no clue, no answer. Find the clue first, then pick the form.

## Module 2: when / if + present
Student's Book Extra (with pp.98–102) · level B1 · an EXTRA module (beyond the book, useful for the entrance exam)

**The key idea:** After when, if, before, after and as soon as, use the present simple — even for the future: I'll call you when I arrive.

Look: *I'll text you when I **get** home.* Getting home is in the future, but we say *get*, not *will get*. The *will* goes in the other half of the sentence.

The same with *if*: *If it **rains** tomorrow, we'll stay at home.* (✗ *If it will rain…*) This is a favourite question in find-the-mistake tests.

Two halves: the *when / if* half uses the present simple; the main half uses *will* (or an instruction like *Call me!*). The halves can swap places. Use a comma when the *when / if* half comes first.

Pattern table:
- when / if half (present simple) | main half (will / instruction)
- When I finish my homework, | I'll play games.
- If it's sunny on Saturday, | we'll go to the beach.
- As soon as the film ends, | I'll call you.
- Before you go to bed, | close the window.

Good examples:
- If we **see** a snake, we'll walk away slowly.
- Text me as soon as you **get** to the station.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "I'll call you when I will arrive." → Right: "I'll call you when I arrive."
- Wrong: "If it will rain, we won't go." → Right: "If it rains, we won't go."
- Wrong: "When she will get home, she'll feed the cat." → Right: "When she gets home, she'll feed the cat."

Extra: Extra: the book's Unit 7 rule box doesn't show this rule, but error-finding tests love it. Spot *when, if, before, after, as soon as* — then check there is no *will* straight after the subject in that half.

Memory tip: When and if hate will. Keep will out of their half of the sentence.

## Quick challenge for the end of the episode
Question: I'm sure (blank) the Unit 7 test next week. You've studied so hard!
Options: (A) you're passing  (B) you pass  (C) you'll pass  (D) you passing
Answer: you'll pass — An opinion about the future (*I'm sure*) is a prediction: *you'll pass*. *You're passing* is for arranged plans — you can't arrange a test result!
