## Module 1: will/won't: the form
Student's Book p.98, p.102 · level A2

**The key idea:** will / won't + base verb — the same for everybody: I will, she will, they will.

*Will* is easy: it never changes. *I'll go, she'll go, they'll go.* After it comes the base verb with no *-s*, no *to* and no *-ing*: *She will **speak** (✗ *will speaks*, ✗ *will to speak*).

Negative: *will not* = *won't* (it rhymes with *don't*, not with *want*). Never use *don't* or *doesn't* with will: ✓ *They won't come.* ✗ *They don't will come.*

Questions: put *will* first: *Will it be sunny?* (✗ *Does it will be…?*) Short answers: *Yes, it will. / No, it won't.* We don't end a short answer with *'ll*: ✗ *Yes, it'll.*

Pattern table:
- Form | Example
- + | subject + will ('ll) + verb | It'll be sunny on Saturday.
- – | subject + won't + verb | The tigers won't come near the road.
- ? | Will + subject + verb? | Will we see a whale?
- Short answers | Yes, … will. / No, … won't. | Yes, we will. / No, we won't.

Good examples:
- My brother **will be** 15 next year.
- **Will** you be at home? — Yes, I **will**.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "She will speaks English very well one day." → Right: "She will speak English very well one day."
- Wrong: "We don't will win the match." → Right: "We won't win the match."
- Wrong: "I will to call you tonight." → Right: "I will call you tonight. / I'll call you tonight."
- Wrong: "Does it will rain tomorrow?" → Right: "Will it rain tomorrow?"

Memory tip: Will is a wall: nothing changes after it. Just the base verb — no -s, no to, no -ing.

## Module 2: Predictions, offers and promises
Student's Book pp.98–99, p.102 · level B1

**The key idea:** Use will for what you think will happen. Extra: also for offers, promises and decisions you make right now.

The book's rule: *will* makes **predictions** — your opinion about the future. Clue words: *I think, I'm sure, probably, maybe, I hope, in 2050*. *I think it'll be hot tomorrow. In 2050, robots will cook our dinner.*

For a negative opinion, we usually say *I don't think … will*: *I don't think Mek will come.* It's the frame on p.99: *I think I'll… / I don't think I will…*

To ask for someone's opinion: *Do you think we'll see an elephant?* A prediction is not a plan and not an arrangement — nobody has decided or booked anything.

Pattern table:
- Clue | Use of will | Example
- I think / I'm sure / probably / in 2050 | prediction (book) | I'm sure you'll love Chiang Mai.
- The phone's ringing! / Oh no, it's dark! | decision now (Extra) | I'll answer it!
- That bag looks heavy. | offer (Extra) | I'll carry it for you.
- I promise… | promise (Extra) | I'll pay you back tomorrow, I promise.

Good examples:
- I'm sure the sharks **won't come** near the beach.
- It's hot in here. **I'll open** the window.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "I think it rains tomorrow." → Right: "I think it'll rain tomorrow."
- Wrong: "“There's someone at the door.” “I go.”" → Right: "“I'll go.”"
- Wrong: "I promise I call you tonight." → Right: "I promise I'll call you tonight."

Extra: Extra: *will* is also for a decision you make at the moment of speaking (*The phone's ringing — I'll answer it!*), offers (*I'll help you with that bag.*) and promises (*I'll call you tonight, I promise.*). The present simple is wrong here: ✗ *I answer it.*

Memory tip: Guessing, offering, promising or deciding right now? No old plan? Then it's will.

## Quick challenge for the end of the episode
Question: I think Mek (blank) the race on Sports Day. He's very fast.
Options: (A) will wins  (B) will to win  (C) will winning  (D) will win
Answer: will win — After *will* we use the base verb with no changes: *will win*. Not *will wins* (no -s), *will to win* (no to) or *will winning*.
