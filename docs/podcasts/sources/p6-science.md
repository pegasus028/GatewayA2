## The pattern
**Fruit in the kitchen, fruit in science**

Cooks and scientists use different groups. In the kitchen, fruit is sweet and we eat it as a snack or a dessert: apples, pears, grapes, melons, strawberries. Vegetables are not sweet; they go in salads and cooking. In science, a fruit is the part of a plant that grows from a flower and holds the seeds — so tomatoes, cucumbers and peppers are fruit! More surprises: a grape is a real berry, but a strawberry isn't; a peanut isn't a real nut (it is in the bean family and grows under the ground); and a mushroom isn't even a plant — it is a fungus. For your tests, use the kitchen groups from the book.

Examples:
- Kitchen: a tomato is a vegetable.
- Science: a tomato is a fruit — it has seeds.
- A peanut grows under the ground, like a bean.
- A mushroom is a fungus, not a plant.

Unit words that follow this pattern: apple, pear, grape, melon, strawberry, tomato, cucumber, pepper, nut, mushroom.
