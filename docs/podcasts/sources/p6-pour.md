## The pattern
**Pour it, spread it, scoop it? Then you can't count it.**

Ask what the food is like in your hand. If you pour it (water, tea, orange juice, lemonade, soup), spread it (butter, jam, honey) or scoop it with a spoon (yoghurt, cream, sugar, salt), it has no clear edges. It is 'stuff', so it is uncountable: no a/an, no -s. If it comes in separate pieces with their own shape (an apple, an egg, a pear, a burger, a biscuit), it is a 'thing', so you can count it. To count stuff, count its container or a piece: a glass of water, a jar of honey, a pot of yoghurt. This test works for most of the unit — the next cards show where English plays tricks.

Examples:
- Pour: some water, some tea, a glass of orange juice.
- Spread: some butter, a jar of jam, honey on toast.
- Scoop: some yoghurt, a spoonful of sugar.
- Hold one: an apple, two eggs, three biscuits.

Unit words that follow this pattern: water, tea, orange juice, lemonade, soup, butter, jam, honey, yoghurt, cream, sugar, salt, apple, egg, pear, burger, biscuit.

## Try it (end the episode with this)
Picture your fridge. Name five things in it and say 'pour', 'spread', 'scoop' or 'hold one' for each.
