## This stage in one sentence
Talk about big and small amounts with a lot of, much and many — and ask How much? and How many?

The stage has 2 modules, in this order: (1) much, many, a lot of; (2) How much? How many?.

## Module 1: much, many, a lot of
Student's Book p.84, p.88 · level A2

**The key idea:** Big amounts: *a lot of* works in every sentence. *Many* (plurals) and *much* (uncountables) are best in negatives and questions.

*A lot of* goes with plural nouns and uncountable nouns, in every kind of sentence: *There are a lot of carrots. There isn't a lot of milk. Do you eat a lot of fruit?*

*Many* + plural countable nouns: *There aren't many eggs. Are there many people?* *Much* + uncountable nouns: *I don't drink much milk. Is there much sugar in it?*

In positive sentences, use *a lot of*: *I spent a lot of money* (✗ *I spent much money*). *Many* in a positive sentence isn't wrong, but it sounds formal — *a lot of* is the safe choice.

Don't forget the *a*: ✗ *lot of*, ✓ *a lot of*. And don't mix them up: ✗ *much eggs*, ✗ *many water*.

Pattern table:
- Plural (eggs) | Uncountable (milk)
- + | There are **a lot of** eggs. | There's **a lot of** milk.
- – | There aren't **many** eggs. | There isn't **much** milk.
- ? | Are there **many** eggs? | Is there **much** milk?

Good examples:
- There's **a lot of** traffic in Bangkok.
- We don't have **much** time.
- Are there **many** students in your class?

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "I have so much friends." → Right: "I have a lot of friends. / I have so many friends."
- Wrong: "There isn't many milk." → Right: "There isn't much milk."
- Wrong: "There are lot of places to visit." → Right: "There are a lot of places to visit."

Extra: With *too* and *so*, *much* and *many* are fine in positive sentences: *You eat too much sugar. There were so many people!*

Memory tip: Not sure? A lot of works with any plural or uncountable noun, in any kind of sentence.

## Module 2: How much? How many?
Student's Book p.85, p.88, p.146 · level A2

**The key idea:** Can you count it? *How many* + plural. Can't count it? *How much* + uncountable. For prices: *How much is it?*

*How many* + plural countable noun: *How many eggs do we need?* *How much* + uncountable noun: *How much water do you drink?* The noun comes straight after: ✗ *How many of eggs*, ✗ *How many egg*.

Short answers: a number (*Three.*), *A lot. / Not many. / Not much.* When no noun comes after *a lot*, drop *of*: *Are there any grapes? — Yes, there are a lot.* (✗ *there are a lot of.*)

Prices: *How much is it? How much are they? How much is that?* = What's the price? Here *much* means money, so you don't need a noun. (✗ *How much price…?*)

Pattern table:
- Question | Short answer
- How many sausages do you want? | Two, please. / Not many. / A lot!
- How much milk is there? | Not much. / A lot.
- How much is this pizza? | It's 120 baht.
- How much are these apples? | They're 60 baht a kilo.

Good examples:
- **How many** glasses of water do you drink a day?
- **How much** is the green curry? — It's 60 baht.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "How many milk do we need?" → Right: "How much milk do we need?"
- Wrong: "How many apple do you want?" → Right: "How many apples do you want?"
- Wrong: "Are there any nuts? — Yes, there are a lot of." → Right: "Yes, there are a lot."

Extra: Look at the noun right after *How much/many*, not the food inside it: *How many **cups** of tea…?* (cups = countable) but *How much **tea**…?*

Memory tip: Many → numbers (1, 2, 3…). Much → amounts. Price → How much is it?

## Quick challenge for the end of the episode
Question: There isn't (blank) sugar in this lemonade. It's not very sweet.
Options: (A) a lot  (B) much  (C) many  (D) lot of
Answer: much — *Sugar* is uncountable and the sentence is negative, so *much*. *Many* is for plurals, and *a lot* and *lot of* each miss a word.
