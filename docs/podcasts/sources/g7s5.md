## Module 1: Statements and questions
Student's Book p.101 · level A2

**The key idea:** Statements: subject + verb + object. Questions: the helper verb (is, do, will…) jumps in front of the subject.

An English sentence is like a train with a fixed order: **who** (subject) → **does what** (verb) → **to what or whom** (object). *Tonkla kicked the ball.* Change the order and you change the meaning: *The ball kicked Tonkla!*

So the object goes after the verb: ✓ *You didn't see me.* ✗ *Me you didn't see.* (Writing bank rule a)

In questions, the auxiliary (helper) verb goes before the subject: *am / is / are, do / does / did, will, can*. *Where **is** the class meeting?* (✗ *Where the class is meeting?*) *What time **does** the film start?* (rule b)

Pattern table:
- Question word | Helper | Subject | Verb …
- Where | is | the class | meeting?
- What | are | you | going to do?
- When | will | the bus | arrive?
- — | Did | you | see the whale?

Good examples:
- My little brother **loves** snakes.
- How **are we** travelling to the museum?

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "Me you didn't see at school." → Right: "You didn't see me at school."
- Wrong: "Where the class is meeting?" → Right: "Where is the class meeting?"
- Wrong: "What time the film starts?" → Right: "What time does the film start?"

Memory tip: Statement: S + V + O. Question: jump the helper verb to the front of the subject.

## Module 2: Adjectives and frequency adverbs
Student's Book p.101 · level A2

**The key idea:** Adjective before the noun: a dangerous snake. Frequency adverb after be (I'm never late) but before other verbs (I always walk).

Adjectives go **before** nouns: *a **huge** waterfall, the **important** things*. In Thai the describing word usually comes after the noun, so this is an easy mistake to make (rule c).

Frequency adverbs (*always, usually, often, sometimes, never*) go **after** the verb *be*: *I am **never** sure. She's **always** happy.* (rule d)

But they go **before** the main verb: *I **always** miss the bus. We **usually** go to Hua Hin in April.* (rule e) ✗ *Always I miss…* ✗ *I miss always…*

Pattern table:
- Rule | ✓ | ✗
- adjective + noun | a dangerous snake | a snake dangerous
- be + adverb | He is never late. | He never is late.
- adverb + main verb | He always walks to school. | He walks always to school.

Good examples:
- There's a **beautiful** lake near my grandma's house.
- It's **usually** very hot in Bangkok in April.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "We saw a snake very long." → Right: "We saw a very long snake."
- Wrong: "I never am late for school." → Right: "I am never late for school."
- Wrong: "Always Ken forgets his homework." → Right: "Ken always forgets his homework."

Memory tip: Be? Adverb after. Other verb? Adverb before. Describing word? Before its noun.

## Quick challenge for the end of the episode
Question: Tonkla [[his phone dropped]] [[in]] the river, [[so]] [[he]] was very sad.
Options: (A) his phone dropped  (B) in  (C) so  (D) he
Answer: his phone dropped — The basic order is subject + verb + object: *Tonkla dropped his phone*. The object (*his phone*) goes after the verb, not before it.
