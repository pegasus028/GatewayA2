## This stage in one sentence
Is it in your diary? Then the -ing form can talk about the future.

The stage has 2 modules, in this order: (1) Present continuous for arrangements; (2) Now or later?.

## Module 1: Present continuous for arrangements
Student's Book p.99, p.102 · level A2

**The key idea:** Is it in your diary? Then use the present continuous: I'm meeting Ploy at Siam at 5 tomorrow.

The present continuous (*am / is / are + -ing*) can talk about the future! The book's rule: we use it for future plans that are **confirmed** — the time is fixed, the tickets are bought, or other people know about it.

It usually has a future time word: *tonight, tomorrow, this weekend, next week, on Friday, at 6 o'clock*. *I'm playing football* = now. *I'm playing football on Saturday* = the future.

Use it to ask about someone's plans: *What are you doing this weekend?* (✗ *What do you do this weekend?*) And don't forget be and -ing: ✗ *I meeting*, ✗ *I'm meet*.

*Going to* isn't wrong for these plans either. But for diary plans English speakers very often choose the present continuous — so practise it!

Pattern table:
- Example
- + | We're flying to Phuket on Friday.
- – | Beam isn't coming to the party tomorrow.
- ? | Are you doing anything on Saturday?
- Time words | tonight · tomorrow · this weekend · next week · on Monday · at 3 pm

Good examples:
- I'm **meeting** Fah at the night market at 7 tonight.
- My parents **are driving** to Hua Hin on Sunday. They've booked a hotel.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "What do you do tomorrow evening?" → Right: "What are you doing tomorrow evening?"
- Wrong: "I'm see the dentist at 4 tomorrow." → Right: "I'm seeing the dentist at 4 tomorrow."
- Wrong: "We going to the cinema on Saturday." → Right: "We're going to the cinema on Saturday."

Memory tip: Diary test: could you write it in a calendar with a time? Then say it with be + -ing.

## Module 2: Now or later?
Student's Book p.99, p.103 · level A2

**The key idea:** Same form, two times: “I'm swimming” can be now or the future. The time words tell you which.

*Shh! I'm doing my homework.* = now. *I'm doing my homework with Mint on Sunday.* = the future. The verb is exactly the same! So look for the clues.

Future clues: *tonight, tomorrow, this weekend, next week, on Friday, at 7 tomorrow*. Now clues: *now, right now, at the moment, Look!, Listen!, Shh!*

No time word? Usually it's about now: *What are you doing?* = right now. *What are you doing tomorrow?* = your plans. This is the P / F (present or future) task on p.103.

Pattern table:
- Sentence | Now or future? | Clue
- Look! The monkeys are eating bananas. | Now | Look!
- We're eating at Grandma's tonight. | Future | tonight
- Is Tonkla playing football at the moment? | Now | at the moment
- Is Tonkla playing football on Saturday? | Future | on Saturday

Good examples:
- I'm **watching** a film now. (now)
- I'm **watching** a film with Ken tonight. (future)

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "I watch a film with Ken tonight." → Right: "I'm watching a film with Ken tonight."
- Wrong: "Listen! The birds sing." → Right: "Listen! The birds are singing."

Memory tip: Same -ing, different time: find the time word first, then decide — now or later?

## Quick challenge for the end of the episode
Question: Fah (blank) Ploy at the bookshop at 10 o'clock tomorrow.
Options: (A) meets  (B) is meeting  (C) is meet  (D) meeting
Answer: is meeting — A diary plan with a time and place takes the present continuous: *is meeting* (be + -ing). The present simple *meets* isn't used for personal plans like this.
