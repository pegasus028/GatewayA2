## The pattern
**Two words in one — and how animals become plural**

Many long words are two short words glued together. Water + fall = waterfall: the meaning adds up. But butter + fly = butterfly and jelly + fish = jellyfish: here the parts lie! A butterfly is not a fly, and a jellyfish is not a fish. You met compounds in Unit 6 too: milk + shake, pan + cake, straw + berry. Plurals follow the end of the word: wolf → wolves (f → ves); fox → foxes (x → add -es); butterfly → butterflies (consonant + y → ies), but monkey → monkeys and valley → valleys (vowel + y → just -s); hippo → hippos and rhino → rhinos (short forms of hippopotamus and rhinoceros just add -s). Two words usually don't change: one jellyfish, two jellyfish; one species, two species.

Examples:
- Three **wolves** and two **foxes** were walking in the snow.
- There are lots of **monkeys** and **butterflies** in the forest.
- Scientists found two new **species** of **jellyfish**.

Unit words that follow this pattern: waterfall, butterfly, jellyfish, milkshake, pancake, strawberry, wolf, fox, monkey, valley, hippo, rhino, species, beach, sky.
