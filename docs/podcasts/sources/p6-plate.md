## The pattern
**Every day, some days, only sometimes**

Put food on a simple healthy-plate scale. Every day: water, fruit and vegetables (apples, carrots, spinach, broccoli), rice or pasta, beans and lentils. Some days: fish, chicken, eggs, yoghurt, nuts. Only sometimes, in small amounts: sweets, crisps, biscuits, soft drinks, burgers, sausages, takeaways — and not too much sugar or salt. Health experts give the same basic message: lots of fruit and vegetables, plenty of water, and less sugar, salt and fat. Now turn the scale into advice with should: 'You should drink more water.' 'You shouldn't eat a lot of crisps.' A healthy diet is about the whole week, not one meal.

Examples:
- You should eat fruit and vegetables every day.
- You shouldn't drink a lot of soft drinks.
- A takeaway is OK now and then.
- Should I have some nuts? — Yes, they're a good snack.

Unit words that follow this pattern: water, apple, carrot, spinach, broccoli, rice, pasta, bean, lentil, fish, chicken, egg, yoghurt, nut, sweets, crisps, biscuit, soft drink, burger, sausage, takeaway, sugar, salt, diet.

## Try it (end the episode with this)
Write down what you ate yesterday. Put each food on the scale, then give yourself one 'should' and one 'shouldn't'.
