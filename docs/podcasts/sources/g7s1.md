## This stage in one sentence
Plans in your head and clouds in the sky: meet the going-to future.

The stage has 2 modules, in this order: (1) be going to: the form; (2) Plans and evidence.

## Module 1: be going to: the form
Student's Book p.94, p.102 · level A2

**The key idea:** be going to = am / is / are + going to + the base verb. Only the be part changes.

Look: *I'm going to visit* Grandma. *She's going to visit* Grandma. *They're going to visit* Grandma. What changes? Only *am / is / are*. The words *going to* and the verb stay the same.

After *going to*, use the base verb (the infinitive): *going to **swim** — not *going to swimming*, *going to swims* or *going to swam*.

Negative: put *not* after be: *I'm not / she isn't / we aren't going to…* Questions: put be before the subject: *Are you going to watch the match?* Short answers use be: *Yes, I am. / No, she isn't.*

Don't forget be! *I going to…* is a very common mistake. People often say /gənə/ when they speak fast, but always write *going to*.

Pattern table:
- Form | Example
- + | I'm / He's / They're + going to + verb | They're going to camp in Khao Yai.
- – | I'm not / He isn't / They aren't + going to + verb | Mek isn't going to play today.
- ? | Am / Is / Are + subject + going to + verb? | Is Fah going to come with us?
- Short answers | Yes, … am / is / are. No, … 'm not / isn't / aren't. | Yes, she is. / No, they aren't.

Good examples:
- We're **going to sleep** in a tent tonight.
- **Are** they **going to come**? — No, they **aren't**.

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "I going to buy a new phone." → Right: "I'm going to buy a new phone."
- Wrong: "He is going to visits the zoo." → Right: "He is going to visit the zoo."
- Wrong: "Are you going to swimming later?" → Right: "Are you going to swim later?"

Memory tip: Three parts, every time: BE + GOING TO + VERB. If one part is missing, the sentence is broken.

## Module 2: Plans and evidence
Student's Book pp.94–95, p.102 · level A2

**The key idea:** Did you decide before now? Then use going to: I've saved my money — I'm going to buy a new football.

The book's rule: we use *be going to* for **plans and intentions**. Ask yourself: *Did I decide before now?* If yes, use *going to*. *I've bought the paint. I'm going to paint my room.*

The plan doesn't need a time, a ticket or other people. It's an idea already in your head: *When I'm 18, I'm going to travel around Thailand.* A bucket list is full of *going to*.

Use it to ask about plans, too: *What are you going to do in the holidays?* And don't use the present simple for a plan: ✗ *I've decided. I buy a bike next month.*

Pattern table:
- Clue in the situation | Use | Example
- I've decided… / I've saved… / I've already bought… | going to (plan) | I've saved 500 baht. I'm going to buy a new game.
- When I'm 18… / bucket list | going to (intention) | I'm going to learn to dive.
- Look! / Be careful! — Extra | going to (evidence) | Look at those clouds! It's going to rain.

Good examples:
- I've already bought the flour. I'm **going to make** a cake tonight.
- Be careful! That glass is **going to fall**!

Classic mistakes and their corrections:
- Wrong: "I've decided. I learn the guitar this year." → Right: "I've decided. I'm going to learn the guitar this year."
- Wrong: "Look at those black clouds! It rains." → Right: "Look at those black clouds! It's going to rain."

Extra: Extra: *going to* also predicts something we can SEE coming now: *Look at the sky! It's going to rain.* / *Be careful! You're going to drop those eggs!* The proof is in front of your eyes.

Memory tip: Going to = the future is already on its way: in your head (a plan) or in front of your eyes (evidence).

## Quick challenge for the end of the episode
Question: The water in the lake is very cold, so we (blank) swim today.
Options: (A) isn't going to  (B) don't going to  (C) not going to  (D) aren't going to
Answer: aren't going to — *We* takes *are*, so the negative is *aren't going to*. *Isn't* is for he, she and it, and we never use *don't* with *going to*.
