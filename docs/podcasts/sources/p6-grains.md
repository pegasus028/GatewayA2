## The pattern
**Tiny pieces: English counts beans, but not rice**

Rice grains, lentils and beans are all small — so why 'some rice' but 'some lentils'? Size doesn't decide; habit does. Very small things that we eat by the spoon or the bowl became one mass: rice, sugar, salt. Pasta comes in big shapes, but it is a mass too, and so is toast. Beans, lentils, nuts, grapes and crisps keep their -s — the book even prints beans, lentils and nuts in the plural. Some vegetables are a mass too: some spinach, some broccoli, some garlic. To count them, name a part: a head of broccoli, a clove of garlic, a slice of toast. Clue: if the word list shows an -s, the word is countable.

Examples:
- a bowl of rice ✓ — two rices ✗
- a bag of lentils ✓ — some lentils ✓
- a clove of garlic ✓ — three garlics ✗
- a slice of toast ✓ — two toasts ✗

Unit words that follow this pattern: rice, sugar, salt, pasta, toast, bean, lentil, nut, grape, crisps, spinach, broccoli, garlic.

## Try it (end the episode with this)
Say each word with 'some': some rice, some beans, some spinach, some nuts… Add -s only where it belongs.
