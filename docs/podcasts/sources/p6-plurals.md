## The pattern
**One -s, three sounds**

Plural -s isn't always /s/. After /p/, /t/ and /k/ sounds it is /s/: grapes, carrots, biscuits, nuts, cups, packets. After most other sounds it is /z/: apples, beans, eggs, pears, onions, bottles, jars, tins. After /s/, /tʃ/ and /dʒ/ sounds it is /ɪz/ — an extra syllable: boxes, glasses, sausages, cabbages. Spelling has rules too: box → boxes and glass → glasses (add -es); tomato → tomatoes (but pizza → pizzas); strawberry → strawberries (consonant + y → ies). And fish usually stays the same: one fish, two fish. The final sound carries the meaning, so say it clearly: one carrot, two carrots.

Examples:
- /s/: grapes, carrots, biscuits
- /z/: apples, beans, eggs
- /ɪz/: boxes, glasses, sausages
- Spelling: tomatoes, strawberries — and two fish

Unit words that follow this pattern: grape, carrot, biscuit, nut, cup, packet, apple, bean, egg, pear, onion, bottle, jar, tin, box, glass, sausage, cabbage, tomato, pizza, strawberry, fish.
