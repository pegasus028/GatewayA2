## The pattern
**Thing + -y = what the day is like**

Eight weather words on p.102 come as pairs: the thing (noun) and the description (adjective). The rain falls; it's a rainy day. To build the adjective, add -y, but check the end of the word first. Most words just take -y: cloud → cloudy, rain → rainy, snow → snowy, storm → stormy, wind → windy. A short word that ends in one vowel + one consonant doubles the consonant: sun → sunny, fog → foggy (the same rule gives hot → hotter, wet → wetter). A word that ends in -e drops the e: ice → icy. And w is never doubled: snowy, not snowwy. Use the noun after the, a lot of or there's; use the adjective after It's or before another noun.

Examples:
- The **sun** is very hot today. → It's **sunny**.
- There's **fog** on the road. → It's a **foggy** morning.
- We slipped on the **ice**. → The path was **icy**.

Unit words that follow this pattern: cloud, cloudy, rain, rainy, snow, snowy, storm, stormy, wind, windy, sun, sunny, fog, foggy, ice, icy.
