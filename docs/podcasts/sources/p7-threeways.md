## The pattern
**It's raining · It's rainy · There's a lot of rain**

English gives you three ways to talk about the same weather. Verb: It's raining (now), It rains a lot in July (often). Adjective: It's rainy, a rainy day. Noun: There's a lot of rain, We get a lot of rain. Here is the surprise: of this unit's weather words, only rain and snow are everyday weather verbs. Nobody says It's sunning or It's winding. Use the adjective (It's sunny, It's windy, It's cloudy) or a noun sentence (The sun is shining, There's a strong wind). Two traps: never put a noun straight after It's (✗ It's rain today), and never use There is with an adjective (✗ There is very windy → ✓ It is very windy).

Examples:
- Look! It's **raining**. (verb: it's happening now)
- It's a **rainy** afternoon. (adjective)
- There's a lot of **rain** in September. (noun)
- ✗ It's sunning. → ✓ It's **sunny**. / The **sun** is shining.

Unit words that follow this pattern: rain, rainy, snow, snowy, sun, sunny, wind, windy, cloud, cloudy, fog, foggy, storm, stormy.
